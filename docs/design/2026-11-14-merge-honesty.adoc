== The question

Hypatia learns one confidence value for each fix recipe. Several steps decide
that 2 findings are "the same" before an outcome reaches that value. This note
asks: when such a step merges 2 different things, must the merge be recorded,
and must the confidence be kept apart?

The note uses one distinction from the trope calculus of the owner
(`trope-checker`, `spec/calculus.adoc`, lines 205 to 214). A merge of 2 things
into 1 has 3 grades:

* `Single`: one thing.
* `Fused(τ)`: 2 things in 1, with a label `τ`. The 2 can be recovered from the label.
* `Conflated`: 2 things in 1, with no label. The 2 cannot be recovered.

The calculus proves that `Conflated` is a two-sided zero of grade composition
(`TB/Grade.agda`, lines 246 to 254): no later step removes it. This note does
not claim that the proof applies to Hypatia. It uses the distinction as a
design rule. See <<limits>>.

== Why it matters

The dispatch tier of a recipe follows from its confidence and its annealing
stage. With a merged subject, a recipe can reach `auto_execute` (0.95 or more)
on the successes of findings that only look like the finding in front of it.
This is the reverse of acceptance A3 of issue 984, which asks that each
learning update rest on evidence for that update.

echo-types gives 2 proved results that bound what a later repair can do:

* A count cannot give back its evidence: exact recovery forces distinct
  residues for distinct inputs (`EchoFiniteResidue.agda`, line 36).
* An aggregation that merges 2 inputs has no canonical inverse
  (`EchoAggregation.agda`, line 210).

So the split cannot be recovered later from the confidence value. If the
pattern is not written at the time of the outcome, it is lost.

== Options

=== Option A: label the merge

Each outcome row, and each receipt of `E0-08`, records the pattern id and the
kind of match (`exact`, `category`, `fuzzy`). The confidence stays one value
for each recipe.

* Result: each merge becomes `Fused(τ)`. An audit can split the outcomes of a
  recipe by pattern. Nothing in the dispatch changes.
* Cost: small. 2 fields, in the one place that writes the row.
* Limit: the risk stays. A recipe can still reach `auto_execute` on merged
  evidence. The record only makes it visible.

=== Option B: keep confidence apart for each pattern

The subject of confidence and of the annealing stage becomes the pair of
recipe and pattern. A new pattern starts at the prior of its recipe.

* Result: evidence for one defect no longer raises the tier for another.
  This is the most correct option for the long term.
* Cost: large. It changes the learning model, the confidence files, the
  readers of the gate and the cross-repo transfer. Evidence for each pair is
  thin, so most pairs stay in `report_only` for a long time.
* Needs: a pattern identity that is stable. The identity of today (30
  characters of text) is not.

=== Option C: A now, B after a measurement

Do option A in the E0 work. Then measure, from the labelled rows, how often a
recipe gets outcomes from more than 1 pattern and how far their success rates
differ. Rule on option B with those numbers.

* Result: no evidence is lost from now on, and the larger change rests on a
  measurement, not on a guess.
* Cost: the risk of option A stays until the ruling on B.

=== Option D: no change

Keep the merge as it is, and record this note as a known gap.

=== Recommendation

Option C. Option B is the most correct for the long term, and option C is the
way to reach it: B needs a stable pattern identity and numbers that do not
exist yet, and A is the step that makes both possible. A departure from B as
the direct choice is on purpose, for that reason.

  category, the cluster that is always 0): 3 small work orders now, or after
  the ruling?
. The novelty gate. Does `:untrained` mean "novel" (fail closed) from now on?

== What this note does not propose

* No new way to decide whether 2 findings are alike. The trope repositories
  have no resemblance relation and no metric on tropes. They give a way to
  record a merge honestly.
* No change to the Bayesian update itself.
* No use of `tropecheck` in Hypatia. A lowering of Hypatia's merge steps to
  the Trope IR is a possible later step. It needs its own design.

[[limits]]
== Limits

* The link between the trope calculus and Hypatia is a reading by the author
  of this note. No proof connects them, and no prover was run for this note.
* The results marked "inferred" have no test. A work order that acts on one
  of them must first show it with a failing test.
* The proof status of the cited Agda results is what their repositories
  record. This note did not check them again.
* echidnabot has the same kind of merge in small form (goals that differ only
  in letter case share one tactic history). It is outside this note.
